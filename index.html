Internet measurement notice

Measuring how ASPA changes route reachability.

ASPAragus is an active IPv6 routing experiment on the PEERING testbed. It compares otherwise similar routes to observe whether networks filter paths that are invalid under Autonomous System Provider Authorization.

If an ICMPv6 probe led you here, no action is required.

01

Small ICMPv6 probes

We send rate-limited Echo Requests from controlled research prefixes. The payload contains the URL of this page so operators can identify the measurement.

02

Paired route comparison

A reachable address is measured from a valid-shaped route and an ASPA-invalid route. Comparing the replies helps identify route filtering without scanning applications or service ports.

03

Limited follow-up

Most targets receive only screening probes. A repeat measurement and ICMP traceroute are used only when the two routes produce a meaningful difference.

04

Operator-friendly

Targets are checked against an exclusion list before probing. Network operators can request exclusion of an address or prefix at any time.

What happened

Why did I receive a probe?

Your IPv6 address appeared in a public Internet measurement dataset as a potentially responsive address. We first test basic reachability through a normal route. Only an address that replies is eligible for the paired experiment.

The measurement uses ICMPv6 only. It does not attempt to authenticate, exploit a service, enumerate open ports, or transmit application data.

Identification

Source prefixes

Measurement traffic can originate from the following IPv6 prefixes:

  • 2804:269c:38::/48baseline route
  • 2804:269c:39::/48prepend control
  • 2804:269c:3a::/48ASPA-invalid route
  • 2804:269c:3b::/48valid-shaped control

Data handling

What is recorded?

We record the target address, timestamps, whether an ICMP reply was received, round-trip timing, and ICMP traceroute hops for selected follow-ups. Public BGP data may be used to associate the address with an origin ASN and prefix.

The research output focuses on aggregate routing behavior and evidence needed to distinguish persistent filtering from transient packet loss.

Contact and opt out

Would you like us to stop probing your network?

Send the IPv6 address or prefix you operate to the research contact. We will add it to the experiment's do-not-probe list. You can also use this address for questions about the methodology or collected data.
